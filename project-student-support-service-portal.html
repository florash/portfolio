Case Study · Service Design / Web Content

Student Support Service Portal

A realistic university and public-sector style portal designed to help students find support services, understand what happens next, and complete common tasks with confidence.

Read Case Study View Live Site More Projects

Problem

Why this project mattered

Student support information is often split across different teams, pages, and service names that are familiar internally but not to students. This project was created to show how a clearer service portal could bring together support content in a way that feels easier to scan, more accessible, and more realistic for public-sector or university digital teams.

Users

Who it was designed for

  • Students who need help quickly and may not know which team to contact.
  • International students navigating local systems and support options.
  • Students facing financial pressure, housing instability, wellbeing concerns, or accessibility barriers.
  • Staff, carers, or advocates helping a student find the right support pathway.

Tools

React Vite Information Architecture Accessibility

Goals

What I focused on

The main goal was to create a credible support portal that felt suitable for ACT Government, ANU, or CIT-style digital content work. I focused on information architecture, service clarity, accessibility, realistic support journeys, and structured content that could later be driven by a CMS.

Information Architecture

How the content was organised

  • Built a clear homepage flow around getting help, browsing services, and finding resources.
  • Separated service pages from support resources so each content type served a distinct user need.
  • Used task-based entry points, filtering, search, and related content to improve movement through the portal.
  • Structured service pages around practical questions: who it is for, how to apply, what documents are needed, and what happens next.

Accessibility

Key considerations

  • Used semantic HTML, visible focus states, and keyboard-friendly interaction patterns.
  • Kept headings, labels, and validation messages plain and direct.
  • Used restrained colours and spacing to support readability rather than visual decoration.
  • Designed all key states including search results, empty states, form feedback, and error handling.

Key Screens

How the portal works across core tasks

The project includes a homepage entry point, task-based navigation, service pages, resource pages, FAQs, search states, and a contact flow designed around realistic next steps.

Outcome

Why this is portfolio-relevant

The final concept shows how I approach content-heavy digital work in a way that is practical, accessible, and user-centred. It demonstrates the kind of structured publishing, service-focused design, and plain-English content thinking that is directly relevant to university and government web content roles. The project is also available as a live prototype so the navigation, search, and content structure can be reviewed in context.
